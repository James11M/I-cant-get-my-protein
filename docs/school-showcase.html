01 · Concept

Can a conversation become a real app?

We started with an idea, no finished specification, and a goal: build a training app together while learning how AI can be used for real-world product design and coding.

1

Idea

Make fitness motivating through targets, games, progression and competition.

2

Conversation

Use ordinary language to explain what the product should do and how it should feel.

3

Working software

Turn those decisions into a prototype, backend and full mobile application.

02 · Basic prompts to get started

The first skill wasn't coding. It was describing the problem.

“Build a simple training app for students that rewards consistency, not just maximum exercise.”
“I want it to feel like an app, not a website. Dark navy, gold highlights, quick to understand.”
“Show a weekly target, streak, XP and a way to catch up missed activity without making it too easy.”
“Give me full copy-paste code so we can keep moving quickly and test it on a phone.”

03 · POC and prompting to build

Prototype first. Architecture later.

Expo Snack let us test ideas quickly in one App.js file. That was exactly right at the start: speed mattered more than perfect structure.

Fast experiments

Progress ring, levels, streaks, history modes, leaderboards, workouts and settings could be changed in minutes.

Phone feedback

We could see whether a design actually felt right on a real screen instead of discussing it abstractly.

POC becomes specification

The approved screenshots and App.js now act as the visual source of truth for the full application.

07 · Features of the app

Designing behaviour, not just screens.

🔥 Streaks

Encourage repeat behaviour and consistency rather than occasional bursts of effort.

⭐ XP & levels

Turn long-term activity into visible progress and give users something to work toward.

↻ Comeback

Give users a controlled way to recover from missed activity without making streaks meaningless.

🏆 Leaderboards

Compete with friends, teams, houses, schools, clubs or workplaces where appropriate.

📈 Tracking

History, measurements, strength progression and trends create a reason to keep logging.

🏫 Optional organisations

Schools are one use case, but the same architecture supports workplaces, clubs, gyms and universities.

10 · Next steps — adoption

Building an app is easier than getting people to keep using one.

The next problem becomes product adoption: who tries it, what makes them return tomorrow, and which features create real value rather than just more complexity?

Small pilot

Start with a controlled group of real users and observe what they actually do.

Retention

Measure whether targets, streaks, social competition and progression make people return.

Feedback loop

Use user feedback + app data + AI-assisted development to choose what to improve next.
